The Watchful Tower

What Ido Portal actually does with Christian McCaffrey — and the pieces worth carrying onto the pickleball court. Plus the Borges texts they read together.

Source: “It Changes You” — Danny Jones Podcast, 2 h 29 m. Timestamps link into the video.

“I train the body until the body cannot go wrong — not until the body goes right. I want the body to be able to go wrong. I want to maintain the choice.” Ido Portal, on why flow state isn’t the goal

Seven takeaways for your training

Each one translated to the court — DUPR climb in mind.

Mind09:05

Keep the watchful tower — flow isn’t the goal

Ido rejects pure flow because immersion automates: “I’ve stood behind world champions and I tell you, nobody’s home.” The state he trains is double — absorbed in the task and watching from the tower, without losing either.

On courtNever drill to autopilot. Keep one thread of awareness watching yourself hit while you hit — presence per rep, not rep count.
Vision50:30

Trust the blur — the periphery is faster than the fovea

“What I encounter often is not a lack of peripheral vision. It is an inability to trust it.” Focusing to confirm is too slow; the periphery is quicker and better in low light. He also ties smooth eye movement to smooth neck movement: “You can’t be smooth if your eyes are not smooth.”

On courtKitchen-line drill: partner holds up fingers off-center while you keep eye contact — call the count without looking. In play: eyes soft on the ball, read paddle and third player as blur.
Space53:45

Map with a glimpse, then go blind

The McCaffrey drill happened on a tennis court: three balls thrown to random spots, one look, then blindfolded retrieval. McCaffrey walked to each — “eight steps, bends down, picks the ball.” Not memory, says Ido: spatial mapping, the internal simulation of the scene.

On courtRun it exactly: three balls dropped, one glimpse, eyes closed, retrieve. You’re training the court model you rely on during overheads, ATPs, and the transition zone.
Sound37:40

Hear the game — rhythm is a trainable sense

His boxing coach would turn away from the heavy bag and shout “I can’t hear the music.” High-level play has a sound signature — you can identify an elite skateboarder with your eyes closed. “Sometimes people are kind of deaf. You can enhance it.”

On courtPickleball is rich here: dink contact and speed-up contact sound different before the ball arrives. Spend a session attending to your partner’s contact sound as the primary read.
Game1:12:00

Play a secret double game

A hidden constraint nobody else knows: nasal-only breathing through a roll, a fixed 4-in / 4-out breath count under pressure, or his rule for athletes — “My job is to make you frustrated. Your job: don’t show me any expression of frustration.” And keep it secret: “Don’t tell the other person. Keep some things just for yourself.”

On courtPick one per match: nasal-only, a breath count between points, zero frustration on the face. It detaches you from the scoreboard and builds composure underneath the skill.
Body1:53:10

Observe before you fix — sensing is releasing

For stubborn tightness or a flawed pattern: pay attention without changing anything. Forcing a stretch on protective tension “opens a can of worms” — the tightness usually guards something else, often the low back. “Observing something, sensing something, is almost the same as releasing it.” And when a bad day hits: investigate the feeling’s actual components — where is it, what is it — and “it disappears many times.”

On courtRebuilding a stroke? Spend sessions purely watching what it does now before imposing corrections. Same for the body: attend to the tight spot daily; don’t attack it.
Practice1:04:40

Find what you suck at — and need no reason

“Don’t find what you’re good at and do that. Everybody knows that. Find what you suck at — because there you have a lot more.” And the deeper cut: results, dopamine, even moving beautifully are side effects; chasing them corrupts the work. “Practice should not have a reason.” He took away every reason he caught himself practicing for.

On courtGive your weakest shot the prime slot in every session. Hold the 5.0 loosely — it’s the side effect, not the point.

The reading room

Ido names Borges as the author he reads with McCaffrey — “a text that will make your head hurt after one page.” He doesn’t say which one; these three are the natural fits, in order. Excerpts below; each links to a free full text (they’re short — “Borges and I” is a single page).

Borges and I

Start here 1957 · 1 page

This is the watchful tower: the split between the private self and “Borges,” the public persona things happen to. Read it, then rewatch minutes 13–18 of the podcast — same idea, different vocabulary.

“The other one, the one called Borges, is the one things happen to… I live, let myself go on living, so that Borges may contrive his literature, and this literature justifies me… I do not know which of us has written this page.”

Excerpt · trans. James E. Irby

Read at poets.org Alt. translation (FSG)

The Aleph

1945 · ~20 min

A single point in a Buenos Aires basement that contains every point in space, seen simultaneously — the peripheral, whole-scene awareness theme taken to infinity.

“The Aleph’s diameter was probably little more than an inch, but all space was there, actual and undiminished. Each thing… was infinite things, since I distinctly saw it from every angle of the universe.”

Excerpt · trans. Norman Thomas di Giovanni

Read the PDF (MIT)

Funes the Memorious

1942 · ~15 min

The cautionary mirror: a man who perceives and remembers everything, and therefore can’t abstract, generalize, or act. Total observation without the tower.

“To think is to forget differences, to generalize, to abstract. In the teeming world of Funes, there were nothing but details, almost contiguous details.”

Excerpt · trans. James E. Irby
