The Problem

A world lost in 2011

When Star Wars Galaxies shut down, something irreplaceable went with it. A world where crafters had reputations. Where your name on a blaster meant something. Where hanging out in a cantina was a valid way to play.

Every MMO since has chased the same formula: you are the chosen one, go save the world.

We're building the opposite.

The Core Fantasy
"I matter here."

Not save the galaxy. Not the chosen one. You're a moisture farmer's kid who got good at making blasters, and people in Mos Eisley knew your name because your blasters hit different.

That's the fantasy.

The Product

A browser-based MMO‑lite

Set in the Battle of Origins universe. Spiritkin wardens explore, battle, craft, and trade in a persistent online world.

No download. No install. No app store.

Just a URL. Open it and you're in the world.

The Hook

Crafting is a career,
not a menu

  • Resources spawn with random quality stats and rotate on timed cycles
  • Crafters chase quality windows — "900 Potency in Crystal Glade right now!"
  • Experimentation is real gambling — risk your materials for higher quality
  • Your name is stamped on every item you make
  • Two identical schematics produce wildly different results based on materials, skill, and luck
  • Nobody has done this since SWG
Progression Philosophy

Horizontal, not vertical

Not "I'm level 50 so I'm god." More like: "I've put 200 hours into weaponsmithing so my Frost Blasters are elite, but I can't craft armor worth a damn."

Breadth vs. depth. Every playstyle is valid:

  • The HunterTracks rare Spiritkin across dangerous zones. Sells premium essences to crafters.
  • The ArtificerMasters a schematic. Builds a reputation. Becomes the name people trust.
  • The MerchantReads the market. Buys low in one zone, sells high in another. Moves product.
  • The RegularHangs out in the cantina. Trades stories. Knows everyone. The social glue.
