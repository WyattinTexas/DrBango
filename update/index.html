06 / Consistency

Consistency in Every Card's Voice

145 ability descriptions standardized -phrasing and capitalization locked

Phrasing Rules

  • "Spiritkin" not "ghost"
  • "Sideline:" / "Win:" prefixes
  • "HP" never "health"
  • "deal" not "inflict"
  • "next roll" not "next turn"
  • Doubles / Triples / Singles capitalized

Capitalization

  • HP, KO, KO'd -always uppercase
  • Once Per Game -title case
  • In Play -capitalized zone
  • max HP -lowercase "max"
  • damage, dice -lowercase

08 / Why Mode G

🌑 Why Mode G Wins

The best solution creates a real decision, not just a penalty

⚖

The Tension

Use Moonstone freely -but now choose: ride your current Spiritkin for its ability, or swap early to cure the sickness?

⚡

Playstyle Impact

Aggressive bench-cyclers barely feel it. Campers who sit on one Spiritkin pay heavily. Rewards dynamic play.

⚙

Still Testable

Mode G is the default, but all modes A-F remain in a dropdown for live balance testing if issues arise.

Tested & approved by Wyatt and Skylar. Moonstone stays powerful but is no longer a free win engine. The sickness mechanic adds depth without removing the card's identity.

09 / Why This Matters

Why This Matters

We don't get a second chance to make a first impression

What Happens If We Ship Too Early

  • A player finds an infinite win loop on day one and posts it online
  • Card text says one thing, the ability does another -trust breaks instantly
  • One unfun Spiritkin poisons the experience -"this game is unbalanced" becomes the narrative
  • Backers who paid $40 feel like they funded a rough draft, not a finished game

What Happens If We Get It Right

  • Every Spiritkin feels intentional -players discover combos, not exploits
  • Card text is clean, consistent, and trustworthy -no confusion mid-game
  • The Moonstone creates real decisions, not free wins -players talk strategy, not complaints
  • We ship something we're genuinely proud to put our names on

We played, broke, and rebuilt all 200 Spiritkin -not just for balance, but to make sure each one is fun. The Moonstone dilemma, the consistency passes, the hundreds of test games -none of this is busywork. It's the difference between a game people try once and a game people can't stop playing.
