How a level works

A level is a list of waves. A wave is a list of groups. A group is a number of one kind of soldier, standing in one shape, walking in from one edge, at one moment in time. That is the whole vocabulary — four things. Everything in this document is those four things repeated: about eleven waves and nineteen groups in every level, a hundred levels deep.

The rug

The toy box

Sits on the left at x 130. You start with 10 lives. Every enemy that reaches the box takes some of them — a rifleman takes one, a tank takes three, the Tin Rex takes six.

The three lanes

Everything walks on past the right edge and heads for the box. Up the middle is the wide centre band. Along the top and along the bottom are the narrow flanking bands — that is how a pincer, an ambush or a surround is built.

The clock

Every group carries a number of seconds. 0 means it walks on the instant the wave starts. 3 means it walks on three seconds later — long enough that you have already committed to the first thing.

Who shows up

Eighteen kinds of figure walk out of that crate across the campaign. The art below is the game's own — these are the actual sprites, at their actual relative sizes.

Reading the four numbers: SHOTS is how many hits from one of your riflemen it takes to put the figure down at level 1 — deeper levels multiply it. SPEED and REACH are in the same units as the rug (your rifleman reaches 198). COSTS is how many of your ten lives it takes if it gets to the box.

03

How they stand

Eleven shapes. Every group in the game uses one of them, and the shape decides almost everything about how the fight feels — how many of them can shoot you at once, how many of them you can hit with one shell, and which one dies first.

Each diagram is drawn from the game's real spacing, marching left toward the toy box.

04

The twenty waves

These are the named patterns. Every one of the 1,142 waves in the campaign is one of these twenty, scaled up — the counts grow with the level, the shape never changes. Learn these and you have learned the game's whole grammar.

Diagrams show the pattern at its authored size — the numbers you see are level-1 counts, before the campaign multiplies them.

05

The level book

Pick any rung. You get the whole level, wave by wave, in order — what walks on, in what shape, from which edge, and how many seconds apart — with a picture of every wave laid out on the rug, and a button that makes it march.

How this document is made.
Every number, count, formation and timing on this page was computed by running the game's own campaign code — levelCfg(), waveFromKey() and spawnGroup(), lifted verbatim out of index.html. The soldier art is the game's own SVG. Nothing here is typed by hand or approximated, so if the campaign changes, this manual is regenerated and is true again.
