Starspell · a design pitch

THE ASCENT

You begin every night standing in a meadow, looking up. Joining a game lifts you into the sky.

for STARSPELL v0.2.0 · /beta3 · sky & transition design v1 · aug 10 2026

Live prototype — tap CAMPAIGN. The whole pitch is in those three seconds. Tap mid-flight to skip · ‹ descends home · ♪ adds crickets, the riser and the arrival chime. Built with the same layer math the Phaser build will use.

— WHY THIS NAILS THE GAME —

  • It gives the night a before. Today the menu floats in the same void as the battle — handsome, but placeless. Once the menu is a place on earth, the sky becomes an event you travel to, not a backdrop you were always in.
  • The beasts were always up there. Every fight is a constellation. Of course you look up at it. Skyrim's skill menu proved the gesture — camera tilts from the person to the stars, and the stars are the interface. We keep the gesture and swap the mood.
  • The game already says it in words. ACT I · THE MEADOW SKY. “Three acts · one long night.” ACT III · THE CROWN OF DAWN. This screen makes the game look like its own writing.

For the players we actually have

STARSPELL's players are mostly girls. That doesn't change the design — it sharpens it: warmth over void, wonder over machinery. Dusk rose and lavender instead of dead black; crickets, fireflies and a crescent moon instead of HUD chrome. Mood targets: Sky: Children of the Light · Monument Valley · Alto's Odyssey at night. House rule for every choice below: dreamy, not techy — wishes, not lasers.

— THE SKY —

One vertical world, three screens tall. The bottom frame is HOME (the meadow at dusk); the top frame is the existing battle sky, ending on exactly the game's background color so the handoff is invisible. The master gradient is the single most important image in the game:

Laws of the sky

  • Never pure black. Anywhere. The zenith is indigo; the ground is violet-black.
  • Stars are jewelry, not dust. Three parallax tiers; colors mix base #cfd8ff with warm #ffe9c9, rare pink #ffd1dc, rare mint #c9fff2. Density ramps from ~12 in frame at the horizon to ~200 at the zenith — the thickening sky is what sells the climb.
  • Twinkle law: at most 3% of stars twinkling strongly at once, never synchronized. Five to seven hero stars in the menu sky get a four-point sparkle — the ones you'd wish on.
  • The moon is low, fat and tilted — a waxing crescent with earthshine, warm #f7e8c8, sitting just above the horizon. It slides out in the first half of the rise. The battle sky has no moon: the beast owns the zenith.
  • Set dressing: two hill silhouettes, ~40 swaying grass blades, a few wildflowers, 10–14 fireflies over the grass, 2–3 cloud wisps with moonlit top edges waiting in the middle band, and the existing aurora kept high — a faint teal hint bleeding into the top of the menu is the tease of where you're going.
  • Grain law: ±1.5 RGB dither per scanline in the gradient texture plus a ~3% noise tile over everything. Smooth dark gradients band hard on OLED; grain is what makes them look expensive. (This page wears the same grain.)

— THE MOVE —

One unbroken camera move, ~2.6 seconds plus a 450ms crossfade. It plays once per run — entering campaign, quick play, daily or versus — never between fights.

Timeline

  • Skippable. Any tap mid-flight jumps to the settle. Respect repeat players.
  • prefers-reduced-motion: no pan — a 400ms veil crossfade.
  • Coming home descends in 1.15s. Defeat doesn't descend — a 350ms fade, and you wake in the meadow. Winning the campaign can one day descend into dawn (see LATER).

— THE SOUND —

  • Crickets on the meadow — two voices of soft bandpass chirps. Instant “standing in grass at night.”
  • The riser: a filtered noise sweep, 300→2600Hz over 2.2s, with low wind underneath.
  • The hush: 150ms of almost nothing at the top. Silence is the frame around the chime.
  • Arrival: the forge voice — 880 + 1318Hz sines — already the prettiest sound in the game.
  • The generative pad is global and survives scene changes — continuity comes free. Everything stays procedural: zero audio files, zero downloads.

— THE BUILD —

Full buildable spec lives in the repo: beta3/SKY-DESIGN.md (next to game.js). Everything maps to existing code.

  1. The ascent. New shared builder ssSkyWorld(scene) (~120 lines): gradient texture + grain, ground, moon, clouds, star tiers with scrollFactors, fireflies — and an ascend() promise that runs the timeline. Home places its existing UI in the bottom band (default scrollFactor 1 means it all scrolls away for free), then this.scene.transition({target:'battle', duration:450, data:{mode,resume}}). Battle is untouched except a 300ms fade-in — it already is the zenith.
  2. Sound & feel. Crickets, riser, hush, chime in audio.js; star stretch; skip-tap; reduced-motion veil; fireflies scatter.
  3. Flourishes. The descent home and defeat-fade; a DAWN gradient after a campaign win (you rose at dusk, fought one long night, and come down at sunrise); versus “rise together” — both players ascend on match found, shared seed, same sky.

Perf: ≤320 star sprites + 14 fireflies, transforms and alpha only, DPR already capped at 2 — well inside what the game draws today. No new assets; the sky stays 100% procedural.

— HOW WE KNOW WE NAILED IT —

  • The menu at rest is a place you'd want to stay — crickets, fireflies, moon.
  • Tap → beast named is one unbroken motion. No pop, no cut you can point to.
  • 60fps on a mid phone · ≤4.5s total · skippable.
  • Zero visible banding on an OLED screen.
  • Any paused frame of the ascent is wallpaper-worthy.
  • A first-time player says something out loud. That's the real test.
