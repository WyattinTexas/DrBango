TRAINING DRILLS
DRILL 01
DINK PATIENCE
Shot Selection Training
Balls arc toward your kitchen. Some pop high (triggers), some stay low (traps). Lock on, read the ball, then decide: attack or reset. Patience wins.
TRIGGER DISCRIMINATION
Ben Johns skill: Never attacks a trap ball. Waits for the green light, then commits 100%.
DRILL 02
TRAJECTORY READER
Predictive Positioning
A ball launches across the court and vanishes into fog. Lock your cursor on where you think it will land — before it arrives. Trust your read.
ANTICIPATION
Ben Johns skill: Pre-positions before the ball arrives. Looks slow because he already knows.
DRILL 03
PADDLE READ
Contact Point Focus
An opponent's paddle flashes at different angles. Lock your eyes on the paddle face, ignore body fakes, and predict where the shot goes.
OPPONENT READING
Ben Johns skill: Watches the paddle face at contact, not the body. Sees through every fake.
DRILL 04
KITCHEN RALLY
Sustained Focus
A continuous dink rally. Lock on, dink back, repeat. Your score is based on QE consistency — same focus on exchange 1 and exchange 20.
FOCUS ENDURANCE
Ben Johns skill: Maintains identical focus across 20-dink rallies without breaking rhythm.
DRILL 05
PRESSURE COOKER
Clutch Performance
Lock on targets as the environment descends into chaos — screen shake, decoys, visual noise. Your QE at Level 5 must match Level 1. Stay ice cold.
PRESSURE RESILIENCE
Ben Johns skill: Same quiet eye at 9-9-2 as at 1-0. Never chokes because his process doesn't change.

BASELINE

THE SCIENCE

Quiet Eye (QE) is the final stable visual fixation on a critical target before initiating a motor action. Coined by Dr. Joan Vickers in 1987, decades of research show that expert athletes hold their gaze significantly longer and more steadily than novices — and that this skill can be trained.

In pickleball, QE applies to: tracking the ball to the opponent's contact point, reading paddle angle before the shot, fixating on the landing zone before you move, and maintaining visual discipline across long dink rallies.
